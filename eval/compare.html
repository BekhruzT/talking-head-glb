Lip-sync eval

What these scores mean

Reference: real human blendshape capture replayed on the avatar with the human's own audio. Generation: drop videos into eval/generation/<caseId>.mp4 (e.g. generation/case01.mp4) and reload — they are picked up by name.

Scores are computed by node eval/metric-probe.mjs and read from eval/scores.js; the videos here are for watching, not for measuring. They are scored off the blendshape streams the videos are rendered from, because the pixels are too lossy to score on: a mouth-box luminance curve tracks its own audio at only r = 0.44, which caps any comparison between the columns well below anything useful.

Timing — does the mouth do the same thing at the same point in the sentence? The two streams are put on a common clock using word boundaries (exact from Azure's synthesiser, recognised for the human recording), then correlated across four articulators that survive the trip between the two different solvers: mouthPucker, mouthShrugUpper, mouthLowerDownRight, mouthClose. Word anchors rather than a fitted warp, because warping one audio envelope onto the other lets a wrong sentence half-fit; on ground-truth anchors it scores 0.03.

Scale. 10 = 0.936, what two Azure renderings of the same words in different styles score — as close as two takes of one sentence get. The deliberately wrong answers land at 0.03 (wrong sentence), −0.07 (reversed) and 0.00 (noise), and every real pair beats every wrong one.

Lag — the shift still left after the words line up, so it is the pipeline moving the mouth early or late within the words. Positive is late. Expressiveness — how far the mouth travels, not when: the generation's excursion over the human's on the same channels, kept out of timing so the two failures never hide each other.

Watching the videos works by double-clicking this file; nothing here needs a server any more.
